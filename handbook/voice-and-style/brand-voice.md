# Brand Voice

The reverse-engineered voice profile for MODULR's writing. Where [Writing Rules](writing-rules.md)
lists the hard *don'ts*, this describes the *do* — how the voice actually moves. Built from real
published posts in [This Should Have Been an Email](this-should-have-been-an-email.md):
"Just keep going," "I talked to 100 founders," and the 7-hooks teardown.

## Who is the voice?

Two operators, signed "Braden & Jon" (sometimes "Braden from MODULR"). Reads as one sharp friend
who runs a real email shop and has seen the data, not a brand account. Conversational, a little
irreverent, generous with the good stuff, allergic to fluff. They've made the mistakes they warn
about and they say so.

## Three pillars

1. **Specific** — numbers and real scenes, never vague claims. "Engagement jumped 38%, 12 new clients in 30 days," not "results improved."
2. **Plainspoken** — short declarative lines, the way you'd say it out loud. "Not one." "The bar is so low. Clear it."
3. **Wry** — dry humor as delivery, not the point. "Cool. Very helpful. That's like telling a chef to 'make it taste good.'"

## Voice IS

- Specific over abstract — dollar figures, percentages, named niches (a blood bank for cats and dogs)
- Short, punchy sentences and one-line paragraphs for rhythm
- Self-deprecating in parenthetical asides ("We've definitely never done that.")
- Direct address — talks *to* the reader, sets challenges ("read the last 5 emails your company sent")
- Confident because it has the receipts (2,000+ emails, 180+ brands, the dataset)
- Willing to dismiss the bad advice out loud before offering the real thing
- Warm at the close — accountability buddy, "send us a reply," human signoff
- Generous — gives the actual playbook away, doesn't tease and withhold

## Voice is NOT

(See [Writing Rules](writing-rules.md) for the hard pattern bans. In short:)

- Hype or buzzwords ("unlock," "revolutionize," "supercharge," "streamline")
- Corporate hedging or committee-speak (the thing it mocks)
- Throat-clearing intros ("In today's fast-paced world," "I've been thinking about...")
- Em-dash-littered AI cadence (one per piece, max)
- Generic motivation with no specific so-what
- Preachy or condescending — it challenges, it doesn't lecture down

## Sentence structure

- Length: **mixed** — long setup lines broken by 2–4 word jabs
- Fragments: **yes**, for emphasis ("Not one." "Yours don't. You win.")
- Contractions: **always**
- Direct address: **yes**, constantly
- Formatting: bold the key claim; one-line paragraphs as beats; italic line for the quote that sets up a takedown; ranked lists for teardowns

## Vocabulary patterns

| You say | Not |
|---|---|
| "keep going / show up consistently" | "stay committed to your content cadence" |
| "the bar is so low. Clear it." | "there's a real opportunity to differentiate" |
| "leaving money on the table" | "suboptimal monetization" |
| "your most valuable owned asset" | "a key channel" |
| "sounds like it was written by a committee" | "lacks a distinct voice" |
| "easy mode" | "a low-effort, high-leverage play" |
| real numbers (38%, 12 clients, 573 subject lines) | "significant," "a lot," "many" |

## Signature phrases

- "Cool. Very helpful." (deadpan dismissal before the real point)
- "Not one." / one-word confirmations as their own line
- "The bar is so low. Clear it."
- "That's the whole trick."
- "Here's what easy mode looks like:"
- "Now go [do the thing]."

## Opening moves

1. Quote the bad/common advice, then dismiss it dryly
2. Drop a specific scene with a number ("I just spent 4 days in Cabo with 100+ founders.")
3. Lead with a reader's own words in italics
4. State the contrarian claim flat, no buildup

## Closing moves

1. A direct challenge for the week ("read the last 5 emails your company sent. Not skim.")
2. Human signoff — "Braden & Jon"
3. A P.S. pointing to one real, useful thing (earned, not pushy)
4. "Now go [rewrite your next hook]."

## Tone variations

- **Friday/nurture nudge:** shorter, warmer, lower stakes ("keep going," accountability-buddy energy)
- **Founder essay:** longer, builds a worldview, lands on a challenge and a soft CTA
- **Data teardown:** ranked patterns, real examples with attribution, "how to use this" at the end

## Gut-check questions

- Is there a real number or named specific here, or am I being vague?
- Did I dismiss the obvious bad version before giving the real one?
- Does this sound like one sharp operator talking, or like a brand?
- Am I challenging the reader or lecturing them?
- Could I cut this sentence and lose nothing? If yes, cut it.
- One em-dash, tops?
