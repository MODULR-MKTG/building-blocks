# IP Pyramid

Source: Jay Acunzo. "Commodity experts talk topics. Influential voices explore a premise."

```
   STORIES      ← Packaging (clarify + connect)
   FRAMEWORKS
   ADVICE       ← How-to-think / how-to
   TERMINOLOGY
   PREMISE      ← Foundation (align + differentiate)
```

## What a premise IS
- An assertion about how the world works
- A belief you make and defend
- Something competitors talk about, but not like you
- The answer to "How do YOU see it?"

## What a premise is NOT
- A positioning statement
- A tagline
- A value proposition

## The committed premise: "Revenue-first email"

**Committed 2026-05-18.**

### What it asserts
A newsletter business exists to generate revenue — through product, service, or sponsorship. Open rates, list size, and engagement are inputs, not outcomes. Every email decision (subject line, send time, segmentation, format, frequency) should be evaluated against whether it moves money, not whether it moves vanity metrics.

### What it does NOT mean
- **Not "short-term always."** A revenue-first email can be a long-term brand play. The premise is about *intent*, not timeline. A brand-building newsletter that compounds into a $5M product launch in year three is revenue-first. A "10 tips" newsletter that hits 60% open rates and sells nothing is not.
- **Not "every email pitches."** Many revenue-first emails make no offer. The question is whether the *system* drives revenue, not whether every send does.

### Why it beat the other candidates
- **"Boring is the bottleneck"** — strong, but it's a *tactic-level* assertion (interesting > boring). Sits better as terminology/POV than premise.
- **"Email is entertainment"** — too narrow; only fits creator/newsletter business, not service/SaaS.
- **"Personality is the conversion lever"** — true but downstream of revenue-first thinking.
- **"Your list is a relationship, not a channel"** — true for everyone; not differentiating.

"Revenue-first email" is the only one that's both **debatable** (most newsletter advice still chases engagement metrics) and **load-bearing** for every downstream framework (MODULR Method, scoring, the conversion guarantee, even pricing — we charge $7k because we measure in dollars, not opens).

### The three motions it implies
Different newsletter business models all qualify as "revenue-first" but with different motions:
1. **Product** — newsletter sells a product (info product, SaaS, ecommerce)
2. **Service** — newsletter generates leads for a service business
3. **Sponsorship** — newsletter sells inventory to advertisers

The premise applies to all three, but the *tactics* diverge. See [Three Motions](three-motions.md) for the full playbook — what each motion optimizes for, how [MODULR Method](../methodology/modulr-method.md) bends per motion, and decision rules for diagnosing which motion a client is playing.

## Why it resolves the [Creator vs Consultant Model](creator-vs-consultant-model.md) tension
Without premise → LinkedIn = generic tips = commodity = attracts fans.
With premise → LinkedIn = exploring/defending worldview = attracts clients who share belief system.
