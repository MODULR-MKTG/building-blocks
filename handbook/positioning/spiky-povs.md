# Spiky POVs

The 4 core beliefs that filter, magnetize, and shape every piece of MODULR content. From the About page; a candidate for the homepage hero.

## 1. Revenue > Vanity Metrics ⭐ STRONGEST
> "We don't care about your open rates. Well, maybe a little. But a 2.7% conversion rate beats a 40% open rate every single time."

- Debatable (marketers obsess over opens)
- Evidence-backed (2.7% vs 0.8% industry)
- Teaches a new frame: revenue per send > engagement

## 2. You Need a Point of View
> "Stop trying to please everyone. Have an opinion. Take a stance. The people who disagree weren't going to buy anyway."

- Challenges "don't alienate customers" wisdom
- Meta (a spiky POV about spiky POVs)
- Pre-qualifies ideal clients

## 3. Human First, Marketing Second
> "Your emails should sound like a person wrote them, not a marketing department. Drop the corporate speak. Write like you talk."

- Contradicts "professional" email marketing
- Actionable
- Explains the *how* of anti-boring

## 4. Boring is a Choice
> "Your competitors send boring emails. Your subscribers expect boring emails. That's exactly why you should send interesting ones."

- Weaker on its own — nobody argues *for* boring
- Best as positioning support, not lead POV

## Committed premise (foundation of [IP Pyramid](ip-pyramid.md))

**"Revenue-first email"** ✅ Committed 2026-05-18

The premise sits beneath the 4 POVs above and underwrites them all. POV #1 ("Revenue > Vanity Metrics") is the direct expression of the premise; POVs #2–4 explain *how* you execute on it.

Long-term plays count. The premise is about intent (does this system drive revenue?), not timeline (does this email pitch today?). The three motions — product, service, sponsorship — each demand different tactics, but all qualify. See [IP Pyramid](ip-pyramid.md) for the full reasoning.
