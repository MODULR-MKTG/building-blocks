# MODULR Values

Four values. They're not poster words — they're how we actually decide things. If a choice doesn't sit right with one of these, we don't make it.

## Own

Ownership is central to everything we do — in our own business and in our clients'.

We own the inputs *and* the outcomes that come from them. We don't hand over a deliverable and call it someone else's problem to make it work. If we touched it, we're on the hook for whether it actually moved revenue.

In practice:
- No "we did our part." We own the result, not the task.
- When something breaks, the first question is "what's ours to fix," not "whose fault is it."
- Clients hire us to take something off their plate completely — so we take it completely.

## Do good

We have the power to influence the environment and the communities around us. We use it.

A two-person company still leaves a wake. We'd rather it be a good one — in how we treat clients, how we treat the people we work with, and what we put into the world.

In practice:
- We don't sell people things that won't work for them, even when they'd buy it.
- We share what we know (this handbook is part of that).
- We'd rather lose a deal than win it by being someone we'd be embarrassed to explain later.

## Default alive

We never over-extend the business to chase profits.

[Default alive](https://www.paulgraham.com/aord.html) means the business survives on its own without needing the next thing to go perfectly. We build to stay alive by default — not to gamble our way to a number.

In practice:
- We don't take on work we can't deliver well just because it pays.
- We don't hire ahead of revenue or sign commitments that need a best-case quarter to honor.
- Slow and solvent beats fast and fragile. We're playing a long game.

## Weird

We're running our own race. We don't have to justify it to anyone.

Most agencies look the same because they're all copying each other. We're not interested. If something is right for us and strange to everyone else, that's usually a good sign, not a bad one.

In practice:
- Publishing our entire operating system in public is weird. We did it anyway.
- We say no to the standard playbook when it doesn't fit how we actually want to work.
- "But that's not how agencies do it" is not an argument that moves us.
